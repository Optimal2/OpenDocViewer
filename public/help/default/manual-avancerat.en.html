More features in OpenDocViewer

This continues the Getting started guide: features that not everyone needs every day, but that are good to know about. Read the section you need.

Compare two pages

Click the compare button in the toolbar (Enable compare mode). The page you are looking at moves to the left with a blue frame, and a second page is shown on the right with an orange frame. To swap the right-hand page: right-click a thumbnail and choose Show to right.

One of the pages is always active – the one you last clicked in. The toolbar and the keyboard control the active page, and the frames around the buttons show which one: blue for left, orange for right.

If you print with the Active page method while comparing, you can choose to include only the active page or Both compare pages. The pages are printed exactly as you see them – rotation, brightness and contrast are included, just as when you look at a single page. With every other print method, the pages are printed as they are in the archive.

Controlling the other page without switching

Hold Shift while you click a button or use the keyboard, and the command applies to the page that is not active. The frames change colour while you hold Shift, so you can see what will happen.

Arrow Left and Arrow Right switch the active page. Arrow Left when the left page is already active ends the comparison – so does the compare button, or Close comparison in the right-click menu on the right-hand page.

Fine-tune zoom, position and rotation

Click Fine tuning in the toolbar to show controls for the displayed page, or both pages in side-by-side comparison. They start hidden. Clicking again hides the controls and fine-edit gestures, keeping your zoom, position, rotation and image adjustments. The selection stays the same when you change modes. Each page also has a Fine-tune disclosure with a summary such as 100.0 % · 0.0°:

  • Zoom in percent with one decimal. The − and + buttons change it by 0.1 % (with Shift: 1 %). The zoom buttons along the top of the page still step by 10 % and keep the fine adjustment.
  • Position: X and Y in pixels. Drag the page with the mouse, or select the move button (four arrows) and use the arrow keys: 1 px per press, 10 px with Shift.
  • Rotation in degrees with one decimal. The ↺ and ↻ buttons turn the page 0.1° (with Shift: 1°). A small counter-clockwise tilt shows as a negative number, for example −0.3. The 90° buttons in the toolbar work as before and keep the fine rotation.
  • Reset (circular arrow) returns the page to 100 %, position 0 and rotation 0.

You can type into the fields with a decimal comma or a decimal point – 12,5 and 12.5 both work. Enter, or leaving the field, applies the value. Esc undoes what you typed, and Arrow Up / Arrow Down step the value (with Shift: ten steps). While the bar is open, dragging the page moves it instead of scrolling, and Ctrl + mouse wheel over the page zooms only that page, 1 % per notch. Close the bar to scroll and zoom as usual again.

Lay the pages on top of each other (overlay)

Small differences are easiest to see when the pages lie on top of each other. Click the overlay button (stacked layers) next to the compare button – it is only there while two pages are compared. The overlay view fills the window and has its own toolbar. Page A (the left page, blue) lies underneath and page B (the right page, orange) on top. Both keep the zoom, position and rotation you set in the compare view.

  • Opacity: the slider sets how visible the top page is, from 0 % (invisible) to 100 % (fully opaque). It starts at 50 %. With the slider selected, Arrow Left / Arrow Right change it by 1 %, with Shift by 10 %, and Home / End go to 0 % and 100 %.
  • Swap (up and down arrows) puts the other page on top. The slider always controls the page on top.
  • Calibrate: the same fine-tune bars as in the compare view, marked A and B, are shown above the pages. Dragging the pages moves the top page, and Ctrl + mouse wheel zooms the top page.
  • Nudge mode (the move button): while it is on, the arrow keys move the top page 1 px (with Shift: 10 px). Click the pages first so that the keys go there.
  • Reset to compare (clock with an arrow) restores the zoom, position and rotation the pages had when you opened the overlay view.
  • Copy calibration copies the settings of both pages as text (JSON), so you can paste them into a support case.
  • Curtain opens the curtain view with the same calibration, see the next section.
  • Close – or Esc – takes you back to the compare view. The calibration is kept: the pages keep their adjustments, and the next time you open the overlay view the opacity and the top page are as you left them.

Print the overlay view: click Print to print one composed page of the visible area with the current opacity, top page and both pages’ zoom, position and rotation. The usual print dialog offers the same options, including direct print, Print via PDF and Save PDF. There is no page range to choose: the result is one page. Printing is logged in the same way as other prints, with a page count of 1.

Drag a border between the pages (curtain)

In the curtain view the two calibrated pages lie in the same place, and a border you drag decides where you see which page: page A on one side of the border and page B on the other. This shows quickly whether lines and text continue unbroken across the border.

  • Open: click Curtain in the overlay view's toolbar. In the compare view the curtain button sits next to the overlay button. When the overlay is enabled, open it first to calibrate the pages – until then the curtain button is grey, and its tooltip explains why. If the overlay is disabled for this installation, the curtain uses the compare view's calibration directly.
  • Horizontal / Vertical chooses the direction of the border. Vertical: the border is upright, page A shows on the left and page B on the right. Horizontal: the border is level, page A shows above and page B below. The labels in the corners show which page, with its page number, is where.
  • Move the border: drag the grip on the border with the mouse or a finger. The border is selected when the view opens, so the arrow keys move it at once: 1 % per press, 10 % with Shift. Each direction remembers its own position while the view is open.
  • Fine tuning: both images have controls for zoom, position, rotation and image adjustments. Overlay and curtain always show these controls when opened, regardless of the normal toolbar selection. Edits stay when you close the view; your normal visibility selection stays unchanged.
  • Close – or Esc – takes you back to where you opened the curtain view: the overlay view or the compare view.

Print the curtain view: click Print to print one composed page of the visible area with the current horizontal or vertical border position and both pages’ zoom, position and rotation. It uses the same print dialog, options, direct print, Print via PDF and Save PDF. The result and the print log page count are both one page; the border handle and toolbar are not printed.

Installation settings: compare.overlay.enabled and compare.curtain.enabled control whether each view can be opened; a disabled view has no entry button. compare.overlay.print.enabled and compare.curtain.print.enabled control each view’s Print button. All four default to true; odv.site.config.js overrides them. Only the boolean false switches a key off. Ordinary side-by-side comparison and its printing remain available. If the overlay is disabled, the curtain uses the compare view’s calibration directly.

An installation can disable fine tuning, overlay and curtain together with fineTune.enabled: false in odv.site.config.js. Ordinary viewing, side-by-side comparison and their printing remain available. The default is true; only boolean false disables the capability.

Document metadata

Right-click a thumbnail or the page and choose Show document metadata. You see the information the system that opened ODV sent with the document – for example ID, name, department, date, number of pages and document ID. Which fields exist depends on that system. The information is for display only and cannot be changed in ODV.

All documents in one table

The Metadata table button in the metadata dialog shows the whole session as a table – one document per row. Handy when you want to see at a glance which departments and dates are included.

Sharper PDF pages

PDF pages are already shown in print quality. If you need to see a detail even more sharply: click HQ in the toolbar (Increase PDF page resolution). The page is redrawn at double resolution and the button turns red. It applies only to that page.

HQ is grey for scanned pages (TIFF/JPEG), because they are already shown exactly as sharp as they were scanned. Hover over the button to see why.

Custom size and default zoom

The 1:X button (Custom size) shows the page at a size you choose yourself – a share of the window width. The arrow next to it opens the settings. There you also choose which zoom every new session should start with (Default zoom/size); your organization decides the default. ODV remembers your choices.

The toolbar button by button

  • Print opens printing. The arrow next to it sets the default, Active page or All pages.
  • Selection opens the selection workspace. Red when a selection is active.
  • Page navigation: first, previous, next, last, and a box for the page number.
  • Zoom: out, in, percentage box, 1:1, fit to screen, fit to width.
  • 1:X: custom size. Compare: two pages side by side. Overlay: only in compare mode – lays the pages on top of each other. Rotate: a quarter turn.
  • HQ: sharper PDF page. Brightness, contrast and reset.
  • Theme, language, help. The light on the help button: green = all is well, yellow = the computer is low on memory, red = error.

Dark theme

The theme menu at the far right has Normal, Light and Dark. In the dark theme the interface turns dark while the document page is shown as usual.

Support information

Turn to your local support first – they know how ODV is installed and set up in your organization, and can pass the case on to the supplier. Two things make it much easier to help you: the version and the diagnostics file. Both are under the help menu → About OpenDocViewer.

Good to include in the ticket:

  • Version and build ID from About OpenDocViewer.
  • The diagnostics file (Download diagnostics JSON). It contains technical information about ODV and the browser – no documents.
  • When it happened (date and time), what you did and which browser you used.
  • The text under Technical details if an error box was shown.

Do not send personal data. Do not attach screenshots or printouts that show real documents, names or ID numbers, unless your organization has explicitly asked for them.

When the session has expired

The box The documents could not be shown correctly has a Technical details link. Expand it to see which pages could not be fetched and why. Include that in the ticket.
