Getting started with OpenDocViewer

OpenDocViewer (ODV) is the window where you read documents when you open them from your organization's portal or archive system. This guide shows what you need day to day: finding the right page, zooming, and printing. It takes a few minutes to read.

Want to do more – select pages, compare two pages, or see every keyboard shortcut? Three more guides cover that, and you can expand them at the bottom of this page. The pictures show a made-up test document with no real data. The screenshots show the Swedish interface; choose English in the Language menu to get the button names used in this guide. Click an image to see it larger – click again to close. Download the guide as PDF.

1. What it looks like

ODV has three parts: the toolbar at the top, the thumbnail pane on the left and the document in the middle. The page you are looking at has a blue frame.

Three words you will see again: a document is a file with one or more pages. A session is all the documents you opened together from the portal or archive system. The thumbnails are grouped by document (DOK 1, DOK 2 … in the Swedish interface).

Back to the portal

ODV is opened from another system – a portal, an archive or a business application. You get back there with that system's own buttons or by closing the ODV tab. What it looks like depends on your organization.

2. Wait until everything has loaded

When you have opened the documents, ODV fetches the pages. Meanwhile the page counter is striped and the print and selection buttons are grey. When the stripes disappear, everything is ready.

Why is the light by the print button off?

The small light by the print button belongs to a quick-print feature that your organization may have turned off. If it is off, that is normal. The light on the help button to the right, on the other hand, shows how ODV is doing: green is good, yellow means the computer is low on memory, red means an error.

3. Find the right page

There are four simple ways, and you can mix them as you like.

  1. The arrow buttons in the toolbar: first, previous, next and last page.
  2. Type the page number in the box between the arrows and press Enter.
  3. Click a thumbnail in the left pane.
  4. Scroll with the mouse wheel. When you reach the bottom edge of the page and keep scrolling, a small gauge fills up – and the next page is shown. The same works upwards.
More ways to browse
  • Arrow Down / Arrow Up or Page Down / Page Up change page. Hold the key down to browse quickly.
  • Home and End go to the first and last page.
  • Hold down the previous or next button in the toolbar to browse quickly.
  • Hold Ctrl and the arrow buttons jump a whole document instead of one page. You can tell because the page navigation gets a dashed frame.

4. Zoom and fit

The zoom buttons are in the middle of the toolbar.

  • The magnifying glasses (Zoom out, Zoom in) zoom out and in. You can also type a percentage in the box between them.
  • 1:1 shows the page at its actual size.
  • Fit to screen shows the whole page at once.
  • Fit to width fills the width – it suits most documents. The button is red when it is selected.
More ways to zoom
  • + and - on the keyboard zoom in and out.
  • The 1:X button shows the page at a size you have chosen yourself, and HQ makes a PDF page even sharper. Both are described in the guide More features at the bottom of the page.

5. Rotate, lighter or darker

Is the page sideways, or is a scanned page too dark or too faint? The buttons on the right of the toolbar help you.

  • Rotate left 90° and Rotate right 90° turn the page a quarter turn.
  • Brightness and Contrast each open a slider. Drag it, or click on the scale.
  • Reset adjustments, next to the sliders, brings brightness and contrast back to normal.

The changes are temporary. Rotation, brightness and contrast only change how the page is shown to you right now. Nothing is saved in the archive, and the next time the document is opened it looks as usual again.

On the printout the changes are included only if you choose the print method Active page – both when you are looking at one page and when you compare two. Every other print method (All pages, Simple range, Custom pages and printing a selection) prints the pages as they are in the archive, without your changes.

Rotate with the keyboard

Ctrl+Arrow Left and Ctrl+Arrow Right rotate the page a quarter turn, just like the buttons.

6. The thumbnail pane

The pane on the left shows every page as a small picture, grouped by document. Click a thumbnail to go there. The buttons at the top of the pane make it wider or narrower, and the crossed-out eye hides it completely.

Every thumbnail has small badges in its corners (the letters come from the Swedish interface):

T – the page's place in the whole session D – the document number S – the page number within the document
Right-clicking a thumbnail

Right-click a thumbnail to get a small menu: show the document's metadata (for example ID, department and date – which fields exist depends on the system that opened the documents), hide the page or the whole document, or show the page on the right in compare mode (see the guide Compare two pages). Hiding pages is called selection and has its own guide: Selection – choose the pages that are shown and printed.

7. Printing

This is how you print the page you are looking at, or all pages.

  1. Click the print button at the far left of the toolbar (or press Ctrl+P).
  2. Choose Active page or All pages under Print method. Have you rotated the page or changed brightness and contrast? Then only Active page puts that on paper.
  3. Fill in Reason and Recipient if the dialog asks for them. Your organization decides which fields exist and which are required.
  4. If the Add copy watermark option is there, leave it ticked if the printout is not an original.
  5. Click Print. ODV prepares the pages and then opens the browser's print window, where you choose a printer as usual.

Active page prints the page exactly as you see it – with rotation, brightness and contrast if you changed them. All pages and the other methods print the pages as they are in the archive, without such changes.

The printout can automatically get a header and a footer with information your organization has chosen – for example date, user, document ID, reason and page number – and a watermark if you left it ticked.

More print options
  • The arrow next to the print button decides whether the dialog opens with Active page or All pages preselected. ODV remembers your choice.
  • Simple range prints from one page to another, for example 2–10. Custom pages takes a list, for example 1-3, 7, 9-5. The order you type is the order they are printed in.
  • Automatic page orientation lets ODV choose portrait or landscape per page. Untick it if everything should be portrait.
  • Continue working moves the preparation box to the bottom so you can keep reading. When it is ready, click Open print.
  • After your first printout, the Reuse latest print settings button appears at the top right of the dialog. It fills in the same choices as last time.
  • Want to print only some pages from several documents? Then make a selection first – see the guide Selection at the bottom of the page.

8. Theme, language and help

At the far right of the toolbar there are three menus. Theme chooses a light or dark look, Language switches between Swedish and English, and Help opens this guide. ODV remembers your choices until next time.

9. If something goes wrong

The pages are shown as warning triangles

The document links from the portal or archive system may be valid for a limited time. If you have had ODV open for a long time, the pages may stop showing and a box appears at the top. Click Reset session and the documents are fetched again. You do not need to reload the browser.

Other common situations

  • The print button is grey. The pages are still loading – wait until the page counter is no longer striped.
  • Pages are missing from the thumbnail pane. A selection is active (the selection button in the toolbar is red). Click the red button at the top of the pane to show all pages again.
  • The page is blurry. If it is a PDF page, the HQ button can make it sharper. Scanned pages are already shown as sharp as they are.
If you need to contact support

Contact your local support, as usual in your organization. Open the help menu and choose About OpenDocViewer. It shows the version, and under Diagnostics you can download a file to attach to the ticket. The file contains technical information about ODV – no documents. If the expired-session box is shown, you can expand Technical details there too. More about this in More features.

Selection – choose the pages that are shown and printed

Important: a selection hides pages in ODV and controls what is printed. The original documents in the archive are never affected.

1. Open the selection workspace 2. Remove pages 3. Add pages back 4. Change the order 5. Take a closer look at a page 6. Save or leave 7. How to tell that a selection is active 8. Print the selection 9. Remove the selection

1. Open the selection workspace

Click the selection button in the toolbar – the one with a list and a tick, directly to the right of the print button (tooltip: Manage the pages shown and printed). The Manage selection workspace opens over the whole window.

Two modes: Documents and Pages
  • Documents is the default. Pages are kept together per document. You can remove and add pages and move them within their document.
  • Pages drops the grouping. Pages from different documents can be mixed in any order you like – for example before printing in a particular order.

2. Remove pages

  1. Mark the pages in the right panel that should not be included. Click one page at a time.
  2. Click Remove selected – the arrow ‹ between the panels. The pages lose their tick in the left panel.
  3. To remove everything and start from scratch: click Remove all («).
Faster ways to mark and remove
  • Shift+click marks every page between two clicks. Ctrl+click adds or removes a single page from the marking.
  • Drag a rectangle over several pages to mark them all at once.
  • Double-click a page in the left panel to add or remove it directly.
  • You do not need to open the workspace at all for single pages: right-click a thumbnail or the page in the main view and choose Hide this page or Hide document. The Delete key hides the page you are looking at, Ctrl+Delete the whole document.

3. Add pages back

Changed your mind? Mark the pages in the left panel and click Add selected (›), or Add all (») to get everything back. The Undo and Redo buttons in the toolbar take back one step at a time.

4. Change the order

Drag a page in the right panel to a new place. In Documents mode you can move pages within their document; in Pages mode you can mix freely. The order in the selection becomes the order of the printout.

5. Take a closer look at a page

Hover over a page and click the eye. The page is shown large, with arrows to browse to the next one. A green edge means the page is included in the selection, and the button at the top adds or removes the page directly. Esc closes.

6. Save or leave

The Save button turns green as soon as you have changed something. Click it when you are done, and then Leave to get back to the document. If you leave without saving, you get a question.

7. How to tell that a selection is active

Back in the document, the selection button is red, the page counter only counts the selection's pages, and the thumbnail pane only shows the pages that are included. At the top of the pane there is a red reset button.

8. Print the selection

Open printing with the print button and choose All pages. When a selection is active, you can choose between Current selection – only the pages in the selection, preselected – and Entire session, which prints everything and ignores the selection.

The Simple range and Custom pages options are not available when a selection is active – the selection then decides what is printed. The rest of printing (reason, recipient, watermark) works as in the Getting started guide.

Good to know: printing a selection prints the pages as they are in the archive. If you have rotated a page or changed brightness and contrast, that is not included here – it only is with the Active page print method.

9. Remove the selection

Click the red button at the top of the thumbnail pane. All pages come back. You can also open the workspace, choose Add all, Save and Leave.

Short checklist

  • A green Save button means you have unsaved changes.
  • A red selection button in the toolbar means pages are hidden.
  • Current selection prints what you chose, Entire session prints everything.
Compare two pages – side by side, overlaid or with a curtain

Good to know: everything in this guide is included from the start, but your organisation may have switched parts of it off – see section 7. If a button described here is missing, that is probably why.

1. Open the comparison and pick the pages 2. Calibrate – so that the pages line up 3. Fine-tune each page 4. Lay the pages on top of each other (overlay) 5. Drag a border between the pages (curtain) 6. Print from the comparison 7. What your organisation may have switched off

1. Open the comparison and pick the pages

Click the compare button in the toolbar (Enable compare mode). The page you are looking at moves to the left with a blue frame, and a second page is shown on the right with an orange frame. The first time it is the same page in both places; later, the page you last compared with comes back on the right. The right page becomes the active one.

Choose which page to show on the right in any of these ways:

  • Right-click a thumbnail and choose Show to right. This also works while comparing is off – it then opens at the same time.
  • Shift+click a thumbnail shows the page in the pane that is not active: on the right when the left page is active (or when the comparison is off – then it opens), on the left when the right page is active. The active page does not change.
  • Arrow Right opens the comparison just like the button – the first time with the page you are looking at on the right, later with the page you last compared with – or makes the right page active if the comparison is already on.

One of the pages is always active – the one you last clicked in. The active page's ring is filled, like a radio button, and you can click a ring to make that page active. The toolbar's page box and browsing buttons, the keyboard and the image adjustments apply to the active page. The frames around the buttons show which page is being controlled: blue for left, orange for right.

Controlling the other page without switching

Hold Shift while you click a button or use the keyboard, and the command applies to the page that is not active – one click or key press at a time. The frames change colour while you hold Shift, so you can see what will happen.

Arrow Left makes the left page active and Arrow Right the right page. Ctrl+Arrow Left / Arrow Right turns the controlled page a quarter turn.

End the comparison with the compare button (Disable compare mode), with Arrow Left when the left page is already active, or with Close comparison in the right-click menu on the right page. What you fine-tuned is still there if you switch comparing on again.

2. Calibrate – so that the pages line up

Two pages to be compared rarely lie exactly alike: one may be slightly shifted, slightly tilted or scanned at a different scale. Calibration is ODV's word for the adjustments you make per page so that they line up – zoom, position and rotation, plus the image adjustments brightness, contrast, grayscale and invert. You make them in the Fine-tune panel (section 3), and they follow the pages between the compare view, the overlay view and the curtain view.

  • The calibration belongs to the page. If you switch page in a pane and then back, the adjustments are still there. A page you have not touched is unadjusted. If you load new documents, everything starts over. Nothing is saved to disk or server.
  • The comparison counts as calibrated once you have opened the overlay view. Until then the curtain button in the toolbar is grey, and its tooltip says Curtain: open the overlay view first – it calibrates the two pages. If you end the comparison, or the right page disappears, you need to open the overlay view again before the curtain can be opened.
  • When you open the overlay view, ODV takes a snapshot of both pages' adjustments. The button Reset to the alignment from the compare view in the overlay view goes back to it.
  • Copy the calibration in the overlay view puts both pages' values on the clipboard as text (JSON), so you can paste them into a support case. ODV does not read the text back.

3. Fine-tune each page

Click Fine tuning in the toolbar (the button with the sliders, next to the compare button). A Fine-tune panel is then shown above each page – both pages in compare mode, or the single page when you are not comparing. The button starts off; switching it on expands both panels. Switching it off hides the controls and gestures again, but the adjustments are kept.

The panel's header shows a summary, for example 100.0 % · 0.0°, and the sign ◐ when the image is adjusted. Click the header to expand or collapse the panel. Expanded, it has four groups:

  • Zoom in percent with one decimal, 10–800. The − and + buttons change it by 0.1 % (with Shift: 1 %). The percentage is applied on top of the shared zoom in the toolbar.
  • Position: horizontal and vertical offset in pixels. Drag the page with the mouse, or select the move button (four arrows) and use the arrow keys: 1 px per press, 10 px with Shift.
  • Rotation in degrees with one decimal. The ↺ and ↻ buttons turn the page 0.1° (with Shift: 1°). A small counter-clockwise tilt shows as a negative number, for example −0.3. The ⟲90 and ⟳90 buttons in the panel turn a quarter turn and keep the fine tuning – 4.6° becomes 94.6° – and so do the 90° buttons in the toolbar and Ctrl+Arrow Left / Arrow Right.
  • Image: brightness and contrast 0–200 (100 = unchanged), grayscale 0–100 (0 = in color) and the Invert checkbox. The steps are 1, with Shift 10.

At the bottom there are three resets: Reset all (layout and image), Reset layout (zoom, position, rotation) and Reset image (brightness, contrast, color). A button is grey when there is nothing to reset. In compare mode the toolbar's reset button does the same as Reset all, and the toolbar's brightness and contrast buttons show the controlled page's value.

You can type into the fields with a decimal comma or a decimal point – 12,5 and 12.5 both work. Enter, or leaving the field, applies the value. Esc undoes what you typed, and Arrow Up / Arrow Down step the value (with Shift: ten steps).

While a page's panel is expanded, you move the page by dragging it instead of scrolling, and Ctrl + mouse wheel over the page zooms only that page, 1 % per notch. Collapse the panel, or switch Fine tuning off, to scroll and zoom as usual again.

The zoom buttons behind the ring and the toolbar's zoom

Each page also has its own zoom buttons, tucked behind the coloured ring at the top left: point at the ring (or tab to it) and they fold out below it – zoom in, the factor (for example ×1.0) and zoom out. Each click changes the page's zoom by ten percent of the base zoom and keeps the fine tuning. The toolbar's Fit to screen, Fit to width, 1:1 and 1:X reset both pages' own zoom but keep position and rotation.

4. Lay the pages on top of each other (overlay)

Small differences are easiest to see when the pages lie on top of each other. Click the overlay button (stacked layers) next to the compare button – it is only there while two pages are compared. The overlay view takes over the whole window, with its own toolbar in place of the usual one. Page A (the left page, blue) lies underneath and page B (the right page, orange) on top. Both keep their calibration, and page A sets the shared zoom.

  • Opacity: the slider sets how visible the top page is, from 0 % (invisible) to 100 % (fully opaque). It starts at 50 %. With the slider selected, Arrow Left / Arrow Right (or Arrow Down / Arrow Up) change it by 1 %, with Shift by 10 %, and Home / End go to 0 % and 100 %.
  • Swap which page is on top (up and down arrows) puts the other page on top. The slider always controls the page on top.
  • Calibrate: the panels A and B above the pages are always expanded here, regardless of the choice in the usual toolbar. Dragging the pages moves the top page, and Ctrl + mouse wheel zooms the top page, 1 % per notch.
  • Nudge mode (the move button): while it is on, the arrow keys move the top page 1 px (with Shift: 10 px). If the cursor is in a field, the keys go there instead – click outside the field first.
  • Reset to the alignment from the compare view restores both pages' zoom, position, rotation and image adjustments as they were when you opened the overlay view.
  • Copy the calibration as JSON puts both pages' settings on the clipboard for a support case. Calibration copied is shown for a moment.
  • Curtain opens the curtain view with the same calibration, see section 5. Print is described in section 6.
  • Close – or Esc, except while the cursor is in a text field – takes you back to the compare view. The calibration is kept, and the next time you open the overlay view the opacity and the top page are as you left them.

5. Drag a border between the pages (curtain)

In the curtain view the two calibrated pages lie in the same place, and a border you drag decides where you see which page: page A on one side of the border and page B on the other. This shows quickly whether lines and text continue unbroken across the border.

  • Open: click Curtain in the overlay view's toolbar. In the compare view the curtain button sits next to the overlay button; it is grey until you have calibrated the pages in the overlay view (section 2). If the overlay is switched off for the installation, the curtain uses the compare view's calibration directly.
  • Horizontal / Vertical chooses the direction of the border. Vertical: the border is upright, page A shows on the left and page B on the right. Horizontal: the border is level, page A shows above and page B below. The view always opens vertical with the border at 50 %, and each direction remembers its own position while the view is open.
  • Move the border: drag the grip on the border with the mouse or a finger. The grip is selected when the view opens, so the arrow keys move it at once: 1 % per press, 10 % with Shift.
  • Fine-tune: the panels A and B are expanded here too and change the same calibration as the compare view and the overlay view – the adjustments stay when you close.
  • Close – or Esc – takes you back to where you opened the curtain view: the overlay view or the compare view.

6. Print from the comparison

There are three ways to get the comparison on paper or as a PDF. All of them use ODV's usual print dialog, so reason, recipient, watermark and the choice between direct print, Print via PDF and Save PDF work as in the Getting started guide.

Side by side: click the print button in the toolbar and choose the Active page method. In compare mode the dialog asks What should “Active page” mean in compare view? – Left / primary page only or Both compare pages. With both, two pages are printed, exactly as you see them: zoom, position, rotation and image adjustments are included. Every other print method prints the pages as they are in the archive.

Print the overlay view: click Print in the overlay view's toolbar. You get one composed page of the visible area, with the pages laid on top of each other as on screen: the lower page opaque, the top page at the slider's opacity, and both with their zoom, position, rotation and image adjustments. The toolbar and the slider are not printed.

Print the curtain view: click Print in the curtain view's toolbar. You get one composed page of the visible area with the border's direction and position as at that moment, and both pages' calibration. The grip, the corner labels and the toolbar are not printed.

For the two views the dialog is simpler: the print method is locked to Active page (greyed out), the Pages and scope section is not shown, and the previous print's settings are not offered. The summary says, for example, Prepare the overlay view of pages 1 and 2 as one page or Prepare the curtain view of pages 1 and 2 as one page. The result is always one composed page, portrait or landscape after the shape of the visible area, with the same header, footer and watermark as other prints, and the print is logged with a page count of 1. The Print button in a view is grey until all pages have loaded, and it is also grey while a PDF is being prepared.

7. What your organisation may have switched off

Everything above is included from the start, but whoever manages the installation can switch parts of it off. If a button described in the guide is missing, that is probably why – ask your local support.

  • Fine tuning, overlay and curtain together. Then there is no Fine tuning button, no panels, no zoom buttons behind the ring and neither of the two views. Ordinary viewing, side-by-side comparison and its printing remain.
  • The overlay view or the curtain view separately. A switched-off view has no button at all – neither in the toolbar nor in the other view. If the overlay is switched off, the curtain uses the compare view's calibration directly, and the curtain button is never grey.
  • Printing from a view. The view remains but has no Print button. Side-by-side printing is not affected.
  • The toolbar's image buttons in compare mode. Normally brightness and contrast show the controlled page's values. An installation can instead let the 90° buttons, brightness, contrast and reset give way while a Fine-tune panel is expanded – the panel has the same controls. The HQ button always remains.

Installation settings in odv.site.config.js, for whoever manages the installation: fineTune.enabled switches off fine tuning, overlay and curtain together. compare.overlay.enabled and compare.curtain.enabled control whether each view can be opened. compare.overlay.print.enabled and compare.curtain.print.enabled control each view's Print button. All five default to true, and only the boolean false switches one off. compare.adjust.toolbarMode is 'mirror' (default: the toolbar shows the page's values) or 'hide' (the image buttons give way while a panel is expanded).

Document metadata

Right-click a thumbnail or the page and choose Show document metadata. You see the information the system that opened ODV sent with the document – for example ID, name, department, date, number of pages and document ID. Which fields exist depends on that system. The information is for display only and cannot be changed in ODV.

All documents in one table

The Metadata table button in the metadata dialog shows the whole session as a table – one document per row. Handy when you want to see at a glance which departments and dates are included.

Sharper PDF pages

PDF pages are already shown in print quality. If you need to see a detail even more sharply: click HQ in the toolbar (Increase PDF page resolution). The page is redrawn at double resolution and the button turns red. It applies only to that page.

HQ is grey for scanned pages (TIFF/JPEG), because they are already shown exactly as sharp as they were scanned. Hover over the button to see why.

Custom size and default zoom

The 1:X button (Custom size) shows the page at a size you choose yourself – a share of the window width. The arrow next to it opens the settings. There you also choose which zoom every new session should start with (Default zoom/size); your organization decides the default. ODV remembers your choices.

  • 1:X: custom size. Fine tuning, compare, overlay and curtain: described in the guide Compare two pages. Rotate: a quarter turn.
  • HQ: sharper PDF page. Brightness, contrast and reset.
  • Theme, language, help. The light on the help button: green = all is well, yellow = the computer is low on memory, red = error.

Dark theme

The theme menu at the far right has Normal, Light and Dark. In the dark theme the interface turns dark while the document page is shown as usual. Below the themes, the Larger toolbar buttons checkbox can be unticked for a lower toolbar with smaller buttons; ODV remembers the choice.

Support information

Turn to your local support first – they know how ODV is installed and set up in your organization, and can pass the case on to the supplier. Two things make it much easier to help you: the version and the diagnostics file. Both are under the help menu → About OpenDocViewer.

Good to include in the ticket:

  • Version and build ID from About OpenDocViewer.
  • The diagnostics file (Download diagnostics JSON). It contains technical information about ODV and the browser – no documents.
  • When it happened (date and time), what you did and which browser you used.
  • The text under Technical details if an error box was shown.

Do not send personal data. Do not attach screenshots or printouts that show real documents, names or ID numbers, unless your organization has explicitly asked for them.

When the session has expired

The box The documents could not be shown correctly has a Technical details link. Expand it to see which pages could not be fetched and why. Include that in the ticket.

Short checklist

  • Wait until the page counter is no longer striped before you print.
  • Check the reason and the scope in the print dialog before you click Print.
  • If you get the expired-session box: click Reset session.
