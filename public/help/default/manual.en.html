Getting started with OpenDocViewer

OpenDocViewer (ODV) is the window where you read documents when you open them from your organization's portal or archive system. This guide covers what you need day to day: finding the right page, zooming and printing. It takes a few minutes to read.

Want to do more? Three more guides cover the rest: Selection, Compare two pages and More features. You will find them in the section Want to know more? at the bottom, and each one is also available as a separate PDF.

The pictures show a made-up test document with no real data. The screenshots show the Swedish interface; switch ODV to English in the language menu (the globe) to see the button names used in this guide. Click an image to see it larger – click again to close. Download the guide as PDF.

1. The three parts of the window

ODV has three parts: the toolbar at the top, the thumbnail pane on the left and the document in the middle. The page you are looking at has a blue frame, both in the middle and in the thumbnail pane.

Two words that come up throughout the guide:

  • Document – one record with one or more pages. If you open several documents at once, each document gets a heading in the thumbnail pane: DOC 1, DOC 2 and so on (DOK 1, DOK 2 in the Swedish screenshots).
  • Session – all the documents you opened together from the portal or archive system. The pages are numbered in one sequence through the whole session.
Back to the system you came from

ODV is opened from another system – a portal, an archive or a business application. You return there with that system's own buttons or by closing the ODV tab. What that looks like depends on your organization.

2. Wait until everything has loaded

Once you have opened the documents, ODV fetches the pages. While that happens, the page box in the toolbar is striped and the print and selection buttons are gray. When the stripes disappear, everything is ready.

At the very start, before the pages appear, you may see the Preparing documents box with a progress bar. If you open a very large number of pages at once, the Large document batch detected box may appear: choose Continue anyway to load everything, or Stop loading. If you go to a page that has not loaded yet, Loading pages… Please wait. is shown for a moment where the page should be.

Why is the light on the print button off?

The small light on the print button shows whether ODV prepares a PDF of all pages in advance, so that printing all pages goes faster. The tooltip calls this the print cache, and it is something your organization may have switched on. If the light is off, that is normal, and you can print as usual. The light on the help button at the far right means something else, see If something goes wrong.

3. Find the right page

Pick whichever way suits you – you can mix them.

  • Click the arrow buttons in the toolbar: First page, Previous page, Next page or Last page. Hold an arrow down to page through quickly.
  • Type the page number in the box between the arrows and press Enter.
  • Click a thumbnail in the pane on the left.
  • Scroll with the mouse wheel. When you reach the bottom of the page and keep scrolling, a small gauge fills up and then the next page is shown (your organization may have turned this off). Scrolling up works the same way.
With the keyboard: page through and jump between documents
  • Arrow Down / Arrow Up or Page Down / Page Up change page. Hold the key down to page through quickly.
  • Home and End go to the first and the last page.
  • Arrow Right does not change page – it opens compare mode. Press Arrow Left twice to close it again (see the guide Compare two pages).
  • With several documents open: hold Ctrl down, and the arrow buttons as well as Arrow Down, Arrow Up, Home and End jump a whole document instead of a page (Page Down and Page Up do not). The page navigation gets a dashed frame to show this.

4. Zoom and fit

The zoom buttons come right after the page navigation in the toolbar. The fit option that is selected is red.

  • Zoom out and Zoom in (the magnifying glasses) change the zoom by about ten percent at a time. You can also type a percentage in the box between them, from 5 to 800.
  • Actual size (1:1) shows the page at 100 %.
  • Fit to screen shows the whole page at once.
  • Fit to width fills the width. It suits most documents and is how the page is shown to begin with, unless you or your organization have chosen something else.

Quickest: double-click the page to switch between Fit to width and Fit to screen.

More ways to zoom
  • + and - on the keyboard zoom in and out. So does Ctrl + the mouse wheel over the page.
  • When the page is larger than the window, you can drag it with the mouse to move around. The mouse pointer then turns into a hand.
  • The 1:X button (Custom size) shows the page at a size you have set yourself, and the HQ button makes a PDF page even sharper. Both are described in the guide More features at the bottom of the page.

5. Rotate the page or adjust brightness and contrast

Is the page on its side, or is a scanned page too dark or too faint? The buttons on the right of the toolbar help you.

  • Rotate left 90° and Rotate right 90° turn the page a quarter turn.
  • Adjust brightness and Adjust contrast each open a slider from 0 to 200, where 100 is unchanged. Drag the slider or click on the scale. The button with the round arrow in the slider box sets the value back to 100. The Adjust brightness or Adjust contrast button in the toolbar stays red as long as its value is not 100.
  • Reset adjustments (the last button in the group) removes the rotation and resets brightness and contrast, all at once.

Important: the changes are temporary. They only affect how the page is shown to you right now – nothing is saved in the archive. ODV remembers them per page for as long as the documents are open; the next time the document is opened it looks as usual again.

Want the changes on paper? Choose the print method Active page when you print (see Print). Every other print method prints the pages as they are in the archive.

With the keyboard: Ctrl+Arrow Left and Ctrl+Arrow Right rotate the page a quarter turn, just like the buttons. The sliders snap to 100 for every value between 80 and 120. For a smaller change, type the value in the Fine-tune panel, described below.

Is the page only slightly tilted? The Fine tuning button (the sliders, right after 1:X) shows the Fine-tune panel above the page. There you can turn the page 0.1° at a time, move and zoom it, and switch it to grayscale or invert its colors. The panel is described in the guide Compare two pages, but it works just as well when you look at a single page. If the button is missing, your organization has switched it off.

6. Use the thumbnail pane

The pane on the left shows every page as a small picture. With several documents open, DOC 1, DOC 2 and so on appear above each document. Click a thumbnail to go to that page.

The buttons at the top of the pane change its width: minimum width, narrower, default width, wider and maximum width. The crossed-out eye (Hide thumbnails) hides the pane. A narrow strip remains at the far left, and the Show thumbnails button at the top of it brings the pane back. You can also drag the edge between the pane and the document.

Every thumbnail has small badges in its corners:

T – the page's number among the pages shown right now D – the document number P – the page number within the document

D and P are only shown when the session has several documents. If you make a selection, T only counts the pages that remain. With several documents open, the list ends with the END marker. In the Swedish screenshots the third badge is S.

Right-click a thumbnail: show to right, metadata, hide

Right-click a thumbnail for a menu with up to four options:

  • Show to right – opens the page next to the one you are looking at. See the guide Compare two pages.
  • Show document metadata – information about the document, such as ID, department and date. Which fields exist depends on the system you came from, and the option is only there when that system sent some along and your organization has not turned the metadata display off.
  • Hide this page and Hide document 1/5 (the document's number and the number of documents) – removes the page or the whole document from view. This is called a selection and has its own guide: Selection.

You get the same menu, minus Show to right, when you right-click the page in the middle.

Signed PDF documents. If a PDF document is digitally signed, a signature symbol is shown on the document's thumbnails (and next to the DOC heading when you have several documents), plus a signature button in the toolbar, unless your organization has turned the signature display off. Click the symbol to see who signed and whether the document is unchanged since it was signed. What the colors of the symbol mean is described in the guide More features at the bottom of the page.

7. Print

This is how you print the page you are looking at, or all pages.

  1. Click the print button (Print) at the far left of the toolbar. Ctrl+P normally works too.
  2. Choose a print method: Active page (the page you are looking at, exactly as it looks) or All pages (every page in the session as they are in the archive, or normally only the pages in the selection if you have made a selection).
  3. Choose a Reason and fill in Recipient if you like. If Reason is a list, a reason is normally already selected (the first one) – check that it is the right one.
  4. Check Add copy watermark if the printout is not an original. The box is normally unchecked to begin with, and your organization may have hidden it.
  5. Click Print via HTML or Print via PDF, depending on which buttons you have. The browser's print window opens, and there you choose a printer as usual.

Your organization decides which fields exist, which are required and which reasons you can choose between. If something required is missing, the dialog shows a red message and does not print until you fill it in. For example, if you choose the reason Other without typing any text, it says Please enter additional details for the selected reason.

The difference between the two buttons: Print via HTML opens the browser's print window straight away. Print via PDF lets ODV create a PDF of the pages first – you see the Preparing print box – and then sends it to the browser's print window. If you have the Save PDF button, it saves that same PDF as a file instead of printing it. Do not press Enter in the Reason or Recipient field if you meant to print via PDF: Enter starts Print via HTML straight away.

The printout can automatically get a header and a footer with information your organization has chosen – for example date, user, reason, recipient and page number – and the watermark if you selected it.

More print options
  • The arrow next to the print button (Print defaults) decides whether the dialog opens with Active page or All pages preselected. ODV remembers your choice.
  • Under Print method there are also Simple range (from one page to another, for example 2–10) and Custom pages (a list, for example 1-3, 7, 9-5, separated by spaces or commas). The pages are printed in the order you type them, and a page may appear more than once.
  • Continue working in the Preparing print box moves it to the bottom of the window so you can keep reading. When the PDF is ready, click Open print.
  • After a print via PDF, the Reuse latest print settings button normally appears at the top right of the dialog (a small icon, a clock with a circular arrow; its tooltip says Fill in the dialog with the same choices as the latest print). It fills in the same choices as last time.
  • Some organizations also have the Automatic page orientation checkbox: each page gets portrait or landscape format according to the page image when you print via PDF.
  • Want to print only some pages from several documents? Make a selection first – see the guide Selection at the bottom of the page.

8. Theme, language and manual

At the far right of the toolbar there are three menus. ODV remembers your choices for next time.

  • Choose theme (the icon shows the current theme): System follows your computer's light or dark setting, Normal and Light are two light looks, and Dark makes the interface dark while the document page is shown as usual. The Larger toolbar buttons checkbox switches between larger and smaller buttons in the toolbar.
  • Choose language (the globe): Svenska or English. All text changes immediately. Does ODV show the wrong language the first time you open it in a browser? Choose the language here, and ODV remembers your choice.
  • Open help menu (the question mark): Manual opens this guide in its own window, and About OpenDocViewer shows the version and contact details.

In the manual window you can search: type at least two characters in the Search the manual field and the matches are highlighted; Enter jumps to the next match. On the left is the table of contents – click a heading to jump there. In a narrow window, open it with the Contents button. The Reload manual from server button fetches the latest version of the guide.

9. If something goes wrong

The pages are shown as warning triangles

The document links from the portal or archive system may only be valid for a limited time. If you have had ODV open for a long time, the pages may stop being displayed and the box The documents could not be shown correctly appears at the top. Click Reset session: the page reloads and the documents are fetched again. If that does not help, close ODV and open the document again from the system you came from.

Other common situations

  • A single page shows a warning triangle and the text Error: The document is corrupted, missing, or in an unsupported format. The other pages work. The file may be damaged or in a format ODV cannot show. Open the document again from the system you came from, and contact support if the error remains.
  • The print button is gray. The pages are still loading – wait until the page box is no longer striped. The button is also gray while a PDF is being prepared for an earlier print.
  • Pages are missing from the thumbnail pane. A selection is active – the selection button in the toolbar is red. Click the red button at the top of the pane (Clear the selection filter and show all pages). If the selection hides every page, ODV instead shows the Show all pages button in the middle of the window.
  • The page is blurry. If it is a PDF page, the HQ button (Increase PDF page resolution) can make it sharper. Pages that are image files, such as scanned images, are already shown as sharp as they are.
  • The light on the help button is orange or red. Orange means ODV is running in a memory-efficient mode. Red means a loading error, or that the memory protection has kicked in. If pages are missing and the box The documents could not be shown correctly is shown, click Reset session there; otherwise contact support.
  • The Something went wrong box appears. Click Try again, and if that does not help, Reload.
  • The toolbar is gone and only thumbnails are shown. The window is too narrow. Below about 600 pixels wide, ODV shows only the thumbnails. Make the window wider and the toolbar and the page come back.
If you need to contact support

Contact your local support in the usual way. Include two things in the ticket:

  1. The version. Open the help menu and choose About OpenDocViewer. The version is at the top.
  2. The diagnostics file. Expand Diagnostics in the same box and click Download diagnostics JSON. The file contains technical information about ODV – no documents.

What else support can make use of is listed in the guide More features.

10. Remember

  • Find the page: type the page number in the box and press Enter, or click a thumbnail.
  • Fit to width suits most documents. Double-click the page to switch to the whole page.
  • Print: wait until the page box is no longer striped, choose Active page or All pages, check the reason and click Print via HTML or Print via PDF.
  • Rotation, brightness and contrast only reach the paper with Active page.
  • Warning triangles instead of pages? Click Reset session.

Selection – choose the pages that are shown and printed

Important: a selection hides pages in ODV and controls what is printed. The original documents in the archive are never affected. The selection is not kept: when you load new documents, all pages are shown again.

1. Open the selection workspace 2. Remove pages from the selection 3. Add pages back to the selection 4. Change the order 5. Preview a page in the workspace 6. Save the selection or leave the workspace 7. How to tell that a selection is active 8. Print the selection 9. Remove the selection

1. Open the selection workspace

Click the selection button in the toolbar – the one with a list and a check mark, directly to the right of the print button. Its tooltip says Manage the pages shown and printed. The workspace opens over the whole window.

The button is gray until all pages have loaded. The tooltip then says Selection management is available when all pages are fully loaded.

Two modes. At the top left you choose how pages may be moved:

  • Documents keeps the pages of each document together. You can remove and add pages and move them within their own document. This mode is preselected when the system you came from supplied details about the documents. Otherwise the option is gray and only Pages is available.
  • Pages drops the grouping. You can then mix pages from different documents in any order you like, for example before printing.
More buttons and controls in the workspace
  • Panel layout: Show both panels, or Show selected pages, which shows only the right panel.
  • Thumbnail size: the slider makes the pages in the panels larger or smaller, from 70 to 320 %.
  • Hide added pages in the left panel hides the pages that are already included, so you only see the ones still left to add. The counter then shows how many remain.
  • The eye in a panel header previews that panel's pages from the first one (see Preview a page in the workspace).
  • Drag the divider between the panels to change their width.
  • The Esc key does the same as Leave.
For whoever manages the installation: the button may be switched off

The workspace is switched off with print.selectionWorkspace.enabled in odv.site.config.js. There is then no selection button in the toolbar, but the right-click menu and the Delete key (see Remove pages from the selection) still work.

2. Remove pages from the selection

  1. Click a page in the right panel that should not be included – it gets a dark frame, and the buttons call it selected. Hold Ctrl and click to highlight more pages, or hold Shift and click to highlight every page between two clicks.
  2. Click Remove selected – the arrow ‹ between the panels. The pages disappear from the right panel and lose their check mark on the left.

Would you rather start from scratch? Click Remove all («) to empty the right panel.

More ways to highlight: Shift, Ctrl, rectangle, double-click
  • Shift+click highlights every page between two clicks. Ctrl+click adds a single page to the highlighted set or removes it from the set.
  • Drag a rectangle over an empty area of the panel to highlight the pages inside it. Hold Ctrl to add them to what you have already highlighted.
  • Double-click a page: in the right panel it is removed, in the left panel it is added – if the page is already included (gray, with a check mark) it is removed instead.
  • Click a document header to highlight the whole document. Double-click the header to remove the whole document (right panel) or add it (left panel; if the whole document is already included it is removed).
  • You can also drag pages from one panel to the other.
Hide pages straight from the document: right-click or Delete

For single pages you do not need to open the workspace at all:

  • Right-click a thumbnail or the page in the middle and choose Hide this page or Hide document 1/5. The numbers are the document's number and the number of documents in the session.
  • Press Delete to hide the page you are looking at, or Ctrl+Delete for the whole document.

The pages disappear at once, without saving. The options appear only when all pages have loaded.

3. Add pages back to the selection

  1. Click a page without a check mark in the left panel – pages without a check mark are not included. Hold Ctrl and click to highlight more pages, or hold Shift and click to highlight every page between two clicks.
  2. Click Add selected (›). The pages get their check mark back and appear in the right panel – in Documents mode at their place in the document.

You can also add a page back by double-clicking it on the left or dragging it to the right panel. Want everything back? Click Add all (»).

Undo in the toolbar reverses your latest change, and Redo applies it again. Only the latest change can be undone, not several in a row. To go back to the last saved selection, use the orange button to the left of Save (see Save the selection or leave the workspace).

4. Change the order

Drag a page in the right panel to a new place. A marker shows where it will land when you let go.

  • In Documents mode you move pages within their own document. Drag the document header to move the whole document. A page that is no longer in its original order gets an orange frame. Hover over it and it says This page is out of the original order within its document.
  • In Pages mode you mix freely, even across documents.

The order in the right panel becomes the order in ODV and on the printout. The number at the top left of each page in the right panel is the page's place in the printout.

If you switch to Documents while the order crosses document boundaries, ODV asks whether you want to restore the original document and page order.

5. Preview a page in the workspace

Hover over a page and click the eye (Show page). The page is shown at full size.

  • A green band at the top means the page is in the selection, a red band that it is not.
  • The button in the band adds (+) or removes (−) the page directly. Space does the same.
  • The arrows at the edges, or the arrow keys, step to the next page in the same panel.
  • Esc or the cross at the top right closes the preview.

The eye in a panel header (Preview selected pages from the first page; on the left Preview available pages from the first page) opens the preview on the panel's first page, so you can page through the whole panel.

6. Save the selection or leave the workspace

  1. Click Save. The button is green while you have unsaved changes, and white with a thin frame when there is nothing to save.
  2. Click Leave. You return to the document with the selection active.

If you leave without saving, the Unsaved changes dialog appears with three choices: Save changes, Discard changes and Continue editing.

The orange button to the left of Save discards your unsaved changes and goes back to the last saved selection. The right panel must contain at least one page before you can save.

7. How to tell that a selection is active

Back in the document, the selection shows in three places:

  • The selection button in the toolbar is red.
  • The page box in the toolbar counts only the pages in the selection, for example 1 / 21. The T badge on the thumbnails shows the page's number in the selection.
  • The thumbnail pane shows only the pages that are included. At the top of the pane there is a red button that removes the selection.

Tip: if you hide the page you are looking at, ODV jumps to the nearest page that is still shown.

8. Print the selection

  1. Click the print button, or press Ctrl+P – normally that opens ODV's print dialog.
  2. Choose All pages under Print method.
  3. Check under Pages and scope that Current selection is chosen (it is preselected), so that only the pages in the selection are printed, in the selection's order. If you want everything, choose Entire session instead.
  4. Fill in the print details and start printing as usual. How that works is described in Print in the Getting started guide.

The Simple range and Custom pages options are not available while a selection is active – the selection then decides what is printed. The pages are printed as they are in the archive: rotation, brightness and contrast are only included when you print with the Active page print method.

9. Remove the selection

Quickest: click the red button at the top of the thumbnail pane (Clear the selection filter and show all pages). All pages are shown again in their original order.

From the workspace: click Add all (»), then Save and Leave. All pages are included again. The selection is only removed if the pages are also in their original order. If you have changed the order, use the red button in the thumbnail pane instead.

If you have hidden every page, for example by hiding the last document, ODV shows the text No pages remain in the current selection instead of the page. Click Show all pages there.

Short checklist for selection

  • A green Save button in the workspace means you have unsaved changes.
  • A red selection button in the toolbar means a selection is active: pages are hidden or in a different order.
  • The order in the right panel is the order in ODV and on the printout.
  • Current selection prints what you chose, Entire session prints everything.
  • The red button in the thumbnail pane removes the selection.
Compare two pages – side by side, overlaid or with a curtain

Tip: everything in this guide is included from the start, but your organization may have switched parts of it off – see What your organization may have switched off. If a button described here is missing, that is probably why.

1. Open the comparison and pick the pages

  1. Click the compare button in the toolbar (Enable compare mode), to the right of the 1:X and Fine tuning buttons. The page you are looking at is shown on the left with a blue frame and a second page on the right with an orange frame – the first time the same page, later the one you last compared with.
  2. Right-click a thumbnail and choose Show to right. The page is shown on the right.

This is how the colors and letters fit together, in the guide and in ODV:

Left pageRight page
Blue frame. Marked L in the thumbnail pane (V in the pictures). Called A in the overlay view and the curtain view. Orange frame. Marked R in the thumbnail pane (H in the pictures). Called B in the overlay view and the curtain view.

The view with the two pages side by side is called the compare view. One of the pages is always active: when you open the comparison with the compare button, Arrow Right or Show to right, it is the right page; after that, the one you last clicked in. The ring at the top left of the active page is filled, and you can click a ring to switch. The page box, the arrow buttons, the keyboard and the image adjustments apply to the active page. The frames around the toolbar buttons show which one: blue for left, orange for right.

More ways to open the comparison and switch pages
  • Click a thumbnail to replace the active page. Shift+click replaces the other page. If compare mode is off, it is switched on.
  • Arrow Right does the same as the compare button. If the comparison is already running, the key instead makes the right page active, and Arrow Left makes the left page active.
  • Hold Shift while you click a button or press a key, and the command applies to the page that is not active – one click or key press at a time. The frames change color while you hold Shift.

End the comparison with the compare button (Disable compare mode), with Arrow Left when the left page is already active, or with Close comparison in the right-click menu on the right page. Your adjustments are still there if you switch compare mode on again.

2. The adjustments follow the page (calibration)

Two pages rarely line up exactly. One of them may be slightly shifted, slightly tilted or scanned at a different scale. So you adjust each page on its own – zoom, position, rotation and image – until they match. ODV calls this calibration.

  • The adjustments belong to the page. If you switch to another page and then back, they are still there. A page you have not touched is unadjusted.
  • They follow the page between the three views: the compare view (side by side), the overlay view and the curtain view.
  • They disappear when you load new documents. Nothing is saved in the archive or on the server.

The curtain button in the toolbar is gray until you have opened the overlay view once – only then does ODV count the pages as calibrated. Its tooltip says Curtain: open the overlay view first – it calibrates the two pages. If you end the comparison, you need to open the overlay view again before the curtain can be opened. See Lay the pages on top of each other and Drag a border between the pages.

3. Fine-tune each page

The Fine-tune panel is the tool for calibration. It sits above each page – both pages in compare mode, or the single page when you are not comparing.

  1. Click Fine tuning in the toolbar (the button with the sliders, next to the compare button). The Fine-tune panel is shown expanded above each page.
  2. Change zoom, position, rotation or image in the panel until the page sits the way you want it. You can also drag the page to move it.
  3. Click Fine tuning again when you are done. The panels are hidden, but the adjustments are kept.

The panel header shows a summary, for example 100.0 % · 0.0°, and the ◐ badge when the image is adjusted. Click the header to collapse or expand the panel. Expanded, it has four groups:

  • Zoom in percent, 10–800. The − and + buttons change it by 0.1 % (with Shift 1 %). The percentage is applied on top of the shared zoom in the toolbar. Clicking Fit to screen, Fit to width, Actual size (1:1) or Custom size, or double-clicking the page, sets the zoom back to 100 % on both the left and the right page, even when compare mode is off. Position, rotation and image are kept.
  • Position: horizontal and vertical offset in pixels. Drag the page, or select the move button (four arrows) and use the arrow keys: 1 px per press, 10 px with Shift.
  • Rotation in degrees. The ↺ and ↻ buttons turn the page 0.1° (with Shift 1°); a counter-clockwise turn shows as a negative number. The outer buttons in the rotation group (tooltip Rotates 90° and keeps the fine tuning) turn a quarter turn and keep the fine tuning – 4.6° becomes 94.6°.
  • Image: brightness and contrast 0–200 (100 = unchanged), grayscale 0–100 (0 = in color) and the Invert checkbox.

At the bottom there are three resets: Reset all (layout and image), Reset layout (zoom, position, rotation) and Reset image (brightness, contrast, color). A button is gray when there is nothing to reset.

In the fields you can type with a decimal comma or a decimal point – 12,5 and 12.5 both work. Enter applies the value, Esc undoes what you typed.

While a page's panel is expanded, you drag the page to move it instead of scrolling, and Ctrl + the mouse wheel zooms only that page. Collapse the panel, or switch Fine tuning off, to scroll and zoom as usual again.

In compare mode the toolbar's reset button does the same as Reset all (layout and image) for the active page. The toolbar's 90° buttons keep the fine tuning, and the brightness and contrast buttons show the active page's value.

The zoom buttons behind the ring and the toolbar's zoom

In compare mode, each page also has its own zoom buttons, tucked behind the colored ring at the top left. Point at the ring (or tab to it) and they fold out below it: zoom in, the factor (for example ×1.0, always with a decimal point) and zoom out. The factor is the same value as Zoom in the Fine-tune panel (×1.1 = 110 %). Each click changes it by 0.1, and any decimals you fine-tuned are kept.

The toolbar's Fit to screen, Fit to width, Actual size (1:1) and Custom size set both pages' factor back to ×1.0 but keep position and rotation.

4. Lay the pages on top of each other (overlay)

Small differences are easiest to see when the pages lie on top of each other. In the overlay view, page A (left, blue) lies underneath and page B (right, orange) on top, and you decide how transparent the top page should be.

  1. Click the overlay button (stacked layers) next to the compare button – it is only there while two pages are being compared. The view takes over the whole window, with a toolbar of its own.
  2. Drag the Opacity slider until you can see both pages. The first time, the view opens at 50 %.
  3. Move page B until it lies exactly on page A: drag the page, or use the B panel above the pages. Ctrl + the mouse wheel zooms the top page.
  4. Click Close (or press Esc) when you are done. You are back in the compare view, and the calibration is kept.

The buttons in the view's toolbar:

  • Swap which page is on top (up and down arrows) puts the other page on top. The Opacity slider always controls the page on top.
  • Nudge mode (the button with arrows around a dot): while it is on, the arrow keys move the top page 1 px per press – not while the cursor is in a field.
  • Reset to the alignment from the compare view: when the view opens, ODV takes a snapshot of both pages' adjustments. The button goes back to it – zoom, position, rotation and image.
  • Copy the calibration as JSON (for support) puts both pages' values on the clipboard as text, so you can paste them into a support case. Calibration copied is shown for a moment.
  • Curtain opens the curtain view with the same calibration, see Drag a border between the pages. Print is described in Print from the comparison.

The panels A and B open expanded here, regardless of the choice in the usual toolbar; click a header to collapse it. The next time you open the overlay view, the opacity and the top page are as you left them.

5. Drag a border between the pages (curtain)

In the curtain view the two calibrated pages lie in the same place, and a border you drag splits the picture: on one half you see page A, on the other page B. That shows at once whether lines and text continue unbroken across the border.

  1. Click Curtain in the overlay view's toolbar. You can also click the curtain button next to the overlay button in the usual toolbar – it is gray until you have opened the overlay view once.
  2. Drag the grip on the border. The grip has keyboard focus when the view opens, so the arrow keys move it straight away: 1 % per press, 10 % with Shift.
  3. Choose Horizontal if you would rather have a border that runs across the page. The view always opens with Vertical and the border at 50 %.
  4. Click Close (or press Esc). You are back where you opened the curtain view from: the overlay view or the compare view.
  • Vertical: the border is upright, page A shows on the left and page B on the right. Horizontal: the border runs across the page, page A above and page B below. Each direction remembers its own position as long as the view is open.
  • The labels in the corners show which page is where, for example A · page 1 and B · page 2.
  • The panels A and B are expanded here too and change the same calibration as the other views.
  • Print is described in Print from the comparison.

If the overlay view is switched off in your installation, the curtain opens straight away, with the compare view's calibration.

6. Print from the comparison

There are three ways to get the comparison on paper or as a PDF. All of them use ODV's usual print dialog. You fill in reason, recipient and watermark as usual – see the section Print in the Getting started guide. At the bottom you choose Print via HTML or Print via PDF, depending on which buttons you have. The Save PDF button is only there if your organization has enabled it.

Side by side – two pages on the printout:

  1. Click the print button in the toolbar.
  2. Choose the Active page print method.
  3. Under Pages and scope there are two options: Left / primary page only (preselected) and Both compare pages. Choose Both compare pages.
  4. Fill in the details and click Print via HTML or Print via PDF.

You get two pages, exactly as you see them: zoom, position, rotation and image adjustments are included. If you leave the preselected Left / primary page only, only the left page is printed – even when the right page is active. Every other print method prints the pages as they are in the archive.

Print the overlay view: click Print in the overlay view's toolbar. You get one composed page of the visible area, with the pages laid on top of each other as on screen: the lower page opaque, the top page at the slider's opacity, and both with their calibration.

Print the curtain view: click Print in the curtain view's toolbar. You get one composed page of the visible area, with the border's direction and position as at that moment and both pages' calibration.

When you print from one of the two views, the dialog is simpler:

  • The print method is locked to Active page, and the Pages and scope section is not there.
  • The summary says, for example, Prepare the overlay view of pages 1 and 2 as one page.
  • The result is always one composed page, with the same header, footer and watermark as other prints. Toolbars, the slider, the grip and the corner labels are not printed.

The Print button in a view appears only once all pages have loaded, and it is gray while a PDF is being prepared.

7. What your organization may have switched off

Everything in this guide is included from the start, but whoever manages the installation can switch parts of it off. If a button described here is missing, that is probably why – ask your local support.

  • Fine tuning, overlay and curtain together. Then there is no Fine tuning button, no panels, no zoom buttons behind the ring and neither of the two views. Side-by-side comparison and its printing remain.
  • The overlay view or the curtain view separately. A switched-off view has no button at all, neither in the toolbar nor in the other view. If the overlay is switched off, the curtain opens straight away, and the curtain button is never gray.
  • Printing from a view. The view remains but has no Print button. The button is also missing while the pages are still loading. Side-by-side printing is not affected.
  • The toolbar's image buttons in compare mode. Normally brightness and contrast show the active page's values. An installation can hide the 90° buttons, brightness, contrast and reset while a Fine-tune panel is expanded – the panel has the same controls. The Increase PDF page resolution button always remains.
For whoever manages the installation: the settings behind this section

The settings live in odv.site.config.js. fineTune.enabled switches off fine tuning, overlay and curtain together. compare.overlay.enabled and compare.curtain.enabled control whether each view can be opened. compare.overlay.print.enabled and compare.curtain.print.enabled control each view's Print button. All five default to true; only the value false switches one off. compare.adjust.toolbarMode is 'mirror' (default: the toolbar shows the page's values) or 'hide' (the image buttons give way while a panel is expanded).

View the document's metadata

Metadata is the information that the system which opened ODV sent along with the document – for example document type, title, department, date, number of pages and document ID. That system decides which fields exist. The information is shown for reference only; it cannot be changed in ODV.

  1. Right-click a thumbnail, or the page itself.
  2. Choose Show document metadata. The option is only there when the document has metadata.
  3. Read the table in the Document metadata dialog: the columns Field, Value and Id. A Raw value line under a value shows the original value behind the text that is displayed.
  4. Close with Close or Esc.

All documents in one table

Click Metadata table in the metadata dialog, or press M while the viewer has focus. The Document metadata overview dialog shows the whole session: one document per row and one metadata field per column. Handy when you want a quick look at which departments and dates are included.

The M key is paused in the same situations as the other shortcuts (see All keyboard shortcuts). If the session has no metadata, nothing happens.

Your organization may have switched off the metadata display. The menu option is then missing and the key does nothing.

Check signatures in PDF documents

If a PDF in the session is digitally signed, ODV shows it in three places:

  • In the thumbnail pane – a symbol and a count next to the document heading (for example DOC 1), when the session has more than one document.
  • On the page thumbnails – a small symbol at the top of every page in the signed file.
  • In the toolbar – a button that shows how many signed documents the session has.

The check starts once the first page has been shown and does not get in the way of your reading.

The color of the symbol summarizes the state of the signature:

  • Green – the content is intact since it was signed.
  • Orange – something deserves a closer look: the document was modified after signing, the signature format is not supported, or the trust of the signature is unknown.
  • Red – the signature is invalid, cannot be read, or the content does not match the signature.

If a document has several signatures, or the session has several signed documents, the symbol shows the most serious state.

  1. Click the signature button in the toolbar – it sits just to the left of the three menus at the far right. The Signatures dialog lists the signed documents with the number of signatures, integrity, trust, signer and time.
  2. Click Details next to a document to see each signature on its own.

In the Signatures dialog you can also click a row to go to the first page of that document; the dialog stays open. You reach the same details by clicking the symbol next to the document heading or on a page thumbnail.

The details dialog shows, for each signature: Signer, Organisation, Issuer, Signing time, Reason, Location, Kind, Integrity, Covers the whole document, Certificate valid, Trust and Format. If the document consists of several signed files, each file gets a tab of its own with the file's name, and the heading below the tabs says, for example, File 1 of 2. A dash means that the signature does not carry that piece of information.

Two lines matter most, and it pays to tell them apart:

  • Integrity answers whether the content is unchanged since signing (Intact, Modified after signing and so on). It is checked in your browser, and nothing is sent anywhere for it.
  • Trust answers whether the certificate chain and revocation status have been checked. Only the server can do that, when the document is opened through ODVGateway and your organization has switched the check on. Otherwise it says Not checked, with an explanation at the top of the signature.

Unknown trust does not mean that the signature is invalid. Look at Integrity to see whether the content is unchanged.

Your organization may have switched off the signature display entirely or only the symbols on the page thumbnails.

For whoever manages the installation: the settings

In odv.site.config.js, metadata.enabled switches off the metadata display, pdfSignatures.enabled the signature display and pdfSignatures.thumbnailPageBadge only the symbols on the page thumbnails.

Make a PDF page sharper

Need to see a small detail on a PDF page more clearly? Click HQ in the toolbar (Increase PDF page resolution). The page is redrawn at double resolution without changing its size on screen, and the button turns red. This applies only to that page and only during the current session. In compare mode the button applies to the active page.

The button is gray when it cannot be used. Hover over it to see why: the page is not a PDF page (an image file, such as a scanned image, cannot get sharper than the original), or the page is already drawn at the highest safe resolution. If the resolution has already been raised for the page, the button is red and cannot be clicked again.

Set a custom size and default zoom

Show the page at your own size. Click 1:X (Custom size). The page is shown at a size you decide yourself: a share of the window width, 70 percent by default.

Click the arrow next to the button to change the share in the Window width field. An empty field means the default applies. Enter saves, Esc cancels.

Under Advanced you can limit the size further: Window height (the page may not be taller than that percentage of the window) and Actual size (no larger than that percentage of the page's actual size). ODV uses the smallest of the limits. Your organization may have other defaults.

Choose how new sessions start. In the same menu, under Default zoom/size, you pick the zoom that pages get when you open new documents: Actual size (1:1), Fit to screen, Custom size or Fit to width. The choice takes effect immediately, and ODV remembers it in your browser.

To undo your choice, click the highlighted option again. Your organization's default then applies, normally Fit to width.

The toolbar button by button

From left to right. Hover over a button to see its name. The button that is currently in effect – for example the page fit in use – is red.

  • Print opens the print dialog. The button is gray while pages are loading and while a PDF print is being prepared. The arrow next to it sets the default scope, Active page or All pages. The small light on the button shows the state of a PDF of all pages that ODV prepares in advance, a print cache that your organization may have switched on. Normally the light is off.
  • Manage the pages shown and printed opens the selection workspace. The button is red while a selection is active and gray until all pages have loaded. The button is missing if your organization has switched the selection workspace off. See the guide Selection.
  • Page navigation: First page, Previous page, the page box, Next page, Last page. Type a page number in the box and press Enter. Hold Ctrl and the buttons move document by document, when the session has several. While pages are loading, the page box shows how far the loading has come.
  • Zoom: Zoom out, the percentage box (5–800 percent; type a number and press Enter), Zoom in. Each click changes the zoom by roughly ten percent.
  • Actual size (1:1), Fit to screen, Fit to width and Custom size (1:X): four ways to fit the page. A fit is red while it is in effect and follows along when you change page. The arrow at 1:X opens the custom size settings.
  • Fine tuning shows or hides the Fine-tune panel above each page. Hiding the panel does not remove the adjustments. The button is missing if your organization has switched fine tuning off.
  • Enable compare mode opens two pages side by side. While two pages are being compared, the buttons for the overlay and the curtain are shown as well, unless your organization has switched them off. All of this is described in the guide Compare two pages.
  • Image adjustments (the frame): Rotate left 90°, Rotate right 90°, HQ (Increase PDF page resolution), Adjust brightness and Adjust contrast (each opens a slider; 100 = unchanged) and Reset adjustments, which clears the rotation and removes the brightness, contrast and color adjustments. Everything applies to the page you are looking at, is remembered per page during the session and is never saved in the document.
  • Signatures is shown only when the session contains a signed PDF document and your organization has not switched off the signature display. See Check signatures in PDF documents.
  • Choose theme, Choose language (Svenska or English) and Open help menu with the options Manual and About OpenDocViewer.

The light on the help button shows the viewer's state:

  • Green – Performance mode active. Everything is as usual.
  • Orange – Memory-efficient mode active. ODV is saving memory, either because the computer is short of memory or because your organization has chosen that mode.
  • Red – Hard memory protection active or Loading error detected. See Contacting support.

In the selection workspace, the overlay view and the curtain view, the toolbar is replaced by that view's own buttons. For a lower toolbar with smaller buttons, see theme and button size.

Choose a theme and button size

Click the theme button, the first of the three menus at the far right of the toolbar (Choose theme). The menu has four themes:

  • System follows your computer's light or dark setting and switches automatically when it changes.
  • Normal is a slightly softer light variant.
  • Light and Dark are fixed.

The button's icon shows which theme is in effect. ODV remembers the choice in your browser. The default is System, unless your organization has chosen something else.

Below the themes is the Larger toolbar buttons checkbox. Checked gives the usual toolbar; unchecked gives a lower toolbar with smaller buttons. The menu stays open so that you see the difference right away, and ODV remembers that choice too.

Contacting support – what they need

Turn to your local support first. They know how ODV is installed and set up in your organization and can pass the case on to the supplier. Two things make it much easier to help you: the version and the diagnostics file. Both are in the help menu under About OpenDocViewer.

  1. Click the help button at the far right of the toolbar and choose About OpenDocViewer.
  2. Read off Version and Build id at the top of the dialog.
  3. Expand Diagnostics and click Download diagnostics JSON. The file opendocviewer-diagnostics.json lands among your downloaded files.

Good to include in the ticket:

  • Version and build id from About OpenDocViewer.
  • The diagnostics file. It contains the version, details about the browser and the computer (such as the number of processor cores and the memory), the viewer's address without query parameters, and ODV's settings. It contains no documents and nothing from the documents.
  • When it happened (date and time), what you did and which browser you used.
  • If the Something went wrong box was shown: click Copy details and paste the text into the ticket.
  • If the box The documents could not be shown correctly was shown: say so, and include what it said under Technical details if that line exists in your installation (see below).

Important: do not send personal data. Do not attach screenshots or printouts that show real documents, names or ID numbers, unless your organization has explicitly asked for them.

Reload resource files in the same dialog fetches the interface texts (the language files) again without restarting the viewer.

The box “The documents could not be shown correctly”

The box appears at the top when the pages can no longer be fetched, usually because the document session has expired. The Reset session button reloads the page so that the documents are fetched again. Normally there are also Reload viewer and Dismiss. The common case is described in the Getting started guide.

If your organization has switched on the Technical details line in the box, expand it. It tells you how many pages failed to load and gives a technical message. Include that text in the ticket.

Short checklist for more features

  • Right-click and choose Show document metadata; M opens the table with all documents.
  • The signature symbol: green = intact, orange = take a closer look, red = invalid. Trust is only checked when your organization has enabled the check on the server.
  • HQ applies only to PDF pages, only to that page and only in this session.
  • 1:X is your own size; Default zoom/size controls how new sessions start.
  • For support: version, build id and the diagnostics file – never personal data.
